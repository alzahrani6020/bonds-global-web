Vaccinated Additions to the Business Model

Based on market review and owner/investor requirements, the business model has been enriched with four new profit-sharing and contractual mechanisms. These mechanisms give the company greater flexibility, reduce capital risk, and accelerate cash turnover.

1. Proportional Distribution Based on Certified Valuation + BOQ

Instead of a fixed 50/50 ratio, each party's share is determined based on the value of their contribution to the final asset. Value is determined through a certified real estate appraisal by a valuer licensed by REGA and a Bill of Quantities (BOQ) signed by an approved engineering consultant.

Practical Example

Building market value after appraisal = SAR 3,000,000. Revival and finishing cost = SAR 1,000,000. Total final asset value = SAR 4,000,000.

  • Owner share = 3,000,000 ÷ 4,000,000 = 75%
  • Company share = 1,000,000 ÷ 4,000,000 = 25%

The ratio can be adjusted with a management premium of 5–10% for the company.

When to use: When the owner rejects fixed ratios, or when the existing building value is relatively high compared to revival cost.

2. Cost Recovery First + 20–25% of Net Profit

When the owner rejects valuation or proportional sharing, the company uses a safer model that guarantees capital recovery first and then a clear profit margin.

  1. Company bears 100% of finishing, management, and marketing costs.
  2. From first unit sales, the company recovers its full costs.
  3. After cost recovery, the company takes 20–25% of remaining net profit.
  4. Owner takes 75–80% of net profit.

Practical Example

Revival cost SAR 1,000,000; total sales SAR 4,000,000; net profit after fees SAR 2,500,000.

  • Company recovery: SAR 1,000,000.
  • Remaining net profit: SAR 1,500,000.
  • Company share (25%): SAR 375,000.
  • Owner share (75%): SAR 1,125,000.
  • Total company return: SAR 1,375,000 (37.5% of net profit).

When to use: When the owner is sensitive to sharing ownership or asset value.

3. Company-First Recovery from First Sales

The company recovers its rights from the first stages of unit sales before the owner. This mechanism gives the company:

  • Faster capital turnover: liquidity from sales directly covers finishing and management costs.
  • Reduced capital lock-up period: compared to traditional development models.
  • Stronger financial protection: the company recovers its costs before profit distribution begins.

Priorities can be arranged so the first 20–30% of unit sales go to recovering the company's cost.

4. Subsidiary Company (SPV) Model per Project

Upon agreement with the owner, a separate subsidiary company (Special Purpose Vehicle — SPV) is established under the parent company. The owner contributes the property as an in-kind share, while the parent company contributes completion costs as a cash share. Every project is placed in a separate company, regardless of size. The main objective: avoid commingling assets belonging to different project owners.

A) Converting Asset and Costs into Shares

The property value plus completion costs are converted into shares of the subsidiary company. Profits are distributed according to each party's shareholding. The property value is determined through a certified appraisal by REGA, and completion costs are determined through a signed Bill of Quantities (BOQ) by an approved engineering consultant.

B) Liquidation Upon Completion

After all units are sold and profits distributed, the subsidiary company is liquidated. There is no ongoing obligation or lingering legal/financial liability for the parent company.

C) Two Profit Distribution Options

  • Proportional sharing: each party receives its share based on the value of its capital contribution.
  • Company takes 20–25% of net profit after costs: used when there is disagreement over valuation, ensuring company recovery and a clear profit margin.

D) Company-First Recovery

The company recovers its costs and agreed profits from the first unit sales. The owner receives his rights only after the company has fully recovered. This protects invested capital and reduces cash lock-up period.

SPV Benefits:

  • Legal and financial risk isolation per project.
  • No commingling of assets belonging to different owners.
  • Easier attraction of external investors per project.
  • Independent sale or financing of each SPV.
  • Greater governance and financial reporting transparency.
  • No ongoing obligations after liquidation.

Administrative Cost: Establishing one SPV costs approximately SAR 10,000–25,000. Recommended for every project, even small ones, to isolate risks.

It is a distressed residential building at an early construction stage, revivable with calculated investment cost and healthy profit margin. 5. Applying Vaccinated Partnership Models

Option 1: Classic 50/50 Mudarabah

  • Company bears SAR 7,252,000 revival cost.
  • Owner contributes land and existing building.
  • Net profit SAR 3,748,000 split equally.
  • Company share: SAR 1,874,000.

Option 2: Proportional Distribution

Assumption: Land and building value after certified appraisal = SAR 4,000,000. Revival cost = SAR 7,252,000.

  • Total value = SAR 11,252,000.
  • Owner share = 35.5%.
  • Company share = 64.5%.
  • Add 5% management premium → Company share ~70%.

Option 3: Cost Recovery + 25% of Net Profit

  • Company recovers SAR 7,252,000 from first sales.
  • Remaining net profit = SAR 3,748,000.
  • Company share (25%) = SAR 937,000.
  • Owner share (75%) = SAR 2,811,000.
  • Total company return = SAR 8,189,000.

Option 4: SPV per Project

  • Establish SPV with capital SAR 7,500,000.
  • Parent company: 70%.
  • Asset owner: 30%.
  • Possible third investor 10–20%.
  • Profit distribution by shares after cost recovery.

6. Competitive Advantages

  • Strategic Location: North Abhur is a key emerging residential/tourism area in Jeddah.
  • Appropriate Size: 18 units + 2 villas serves diverse segments.
  • Ready Infrastructure: Parking, services, entrances.
  • Low Completion (~20%): Allows modern design and smart technologies from start.
  • Mortgage Financing: Ready units are easier to finance than off-plan.

7. Risks & Mitigation

8. Lessons Learned

  1. Medium-sized projects (1,000–2,500 m²) balance capital requirement and return.
  2. Low completion rate (~20%) gives more design and marketing flexibility.
  3. Diverse unit mix reduces unsold inventory risk.
  4. Vaccinated partnership models increase offer attractiveness.
  5. 6–8 month payback is excellent in real estate.
Conclusion: The Abhur project proves the model's real-world feasibility. With a cost of ~SAR 7.25M and expected revenue of ~SAR 11M, it achieves ~34% profit margin and ~26% return on company capital within 6–8 months. This makes it an ideal candidate for the SAR 25M pilot phase.
