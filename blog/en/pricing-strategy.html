Pricing Strategy

How to Price Your Product Without Losing Money

️ Bonds Global Team May 2026 ⏱️ 8 min read
Home / Insights / Pricing Strategy

Pricing isn't just a number you put on a product — it's a strategic decision that determines your profit margin, market position, and growth potential. Many factory owners and traders in Egypt and Saudi Arabia are losing money without realizing that the cause is incorrect pricing.

In this guide, we explain the two fundamental pricing methods, the hidden costs you must include, and how to compare yourself against competitors.

Cost-Plus Pricing

This is the most common method: calculate the product cost, then add a profit margin (markup) on top. It's simple and direct, but carries risks if you don't include all costs.

What Is the True Cost?

The biggest mistake is calculating "raw material cost" only. True cost includes:

  • Direct costs: raw materials, production wages, packaging, shipping to customer.
  • Indirect costs: factory rent, electricity, equipment maintenance, management salaries.
  • Hidden costs: production waste, returns, marketing, commissions, taxes, payment gateway fees.
Unit Cost =
(Total Direct Costs + Total Indirect Costs + Hidden Costs) ÷ Number of Units Produced

Markup Formula:

Selling Price = Unit Cost × (1 + Desired Margin Percentage)

Example: If unit cost is 80 SAR and desired margin is 40%:
Selling Price = 80 × 1.40 = 112 SAR

Value-Based Pricing

Instead of starting with cost, you start with the value the customer receives. This model suits distinctive products and services that solve a real customer problem.

How to Calculate Value?

  1. What problem do you solve? The bigger the problem (time, money loss, risk), the higher the value.
  2. What does the customer pay for alternatives? If the alternative costs them 500 SAR, your product at 350 SAR represents value.
  3. What is the customer's return on investment? If you save them 10,000 SAR annually, paying 2,000 SAR for that is fair.
️ Important Warning

Don't use value-based pricing if you can't clearly articulate the value to the customer. In competitive markets (like consumer goods), cost-plus may be safer.

Competitor Analysis: Don't Undercut or Overprice Blindly

Comparing your prices to competitors is essential, but it should not be your only rule. Here's how to do it smartly:

  • Define your competitor category precisely: Don't compare your premium product to a cheap, low-quality alternative.
  • Understand their cost structure: If a competitor sells lower, do they have lower costs (bigger volume, different location) or are they losing money to attract customers?
  • Don't enter a price war: Cutting prices is easy, but raising them again is very hard. Compete on value, not price.
  • Test price elasticity: Try raising prices by 5% on a small customer group and observe the impact on sales.

Recommended Pricing Structure

For factory owners and traders in Egypt and Saudi Arabia, we recommend a flexible pricing structure:

  1. Floor Price: Unit cost + 10% buffer — never sell below this price.
  2. Target Price: Unit cost + target margin (30-50% depending on sector).
  3. Premium Price: For customized offers or customers seeking additional value.

Conclusion

Correct pricing starts with understanding your true cost — all of it — then adding the value you provide, then balancing what the market wants with what you need to survive and grow. Don't price with emotion or blind imitation. Price with numbers.
