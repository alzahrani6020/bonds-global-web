4. Business Model

4.1 Smart Mudarabah Mechanism

Original Owner (owns the distressed building) ↓ Provides: Land + existing structural frame ↓ Real Estate Asset Revitalization Company Provides: Capital for finishing + management + marketing + sales ↓ Profit sharing according to agreed ratio (typically 50/50 or 60/40)

4.2 Revenue Models and Profit Distribution

ModelDescriptionExpected Margin
Traditional MudarabahProfit sharing with the owner (50/50 or 60/40)40–50% of net profit for the company
Acquisition ModelBuying the building outright then reselling15–20% gross margin
Management ModelManaging finishing for a management fee8–12% of finishing cost
Proportional Sharing ModelProfit distributed according to the ratio of building value (before finishing) to finishing costDetermined automatically by valuation
Profit-After-Cost ModelCompany recovers costs then takes 20–25% of net profit (when owner disputes valuation)20–25% of net profit

4.3 Company Priority in Recovery

Priority Recovery for the Company
  • The company recovers all agreed costs from the first unit sales.
  • Then it recovers its agreed profit share (Mudarabah percentage or 20–25% depending on the model).
  • Only after the company has fully recovered its rights does the owner begin to recover his share.
  • Objective: faster capital turnover and reduced cash freezing period.

4.4 Project Subsidiary Model (SPV)

Upon agreement with the owner, a separate subsidiary is established by the parent company, with each project in an independent legal entity regardless of size.

Contribution Mechanism in the Subsidiary

PartyContributionLegal Form
Original OwnerThe property (land + existing building)In-kind contribution to the subsidiary's share capital
Parent CompanyCompletion, finishing, management, and marketing costsCash contribution to the subsidiary's share capital

Conversion of Property and Costs into Shares

  • The property value is determined by an accredited real estate appraiser before contracting.
  • The completion cost is determined by a bill of quantities from an accredited engineering office.
  • The property value + completion cost are converted into shares in the subsidiary.
  • Profits are distributed according to the shareholding percentage of each party.

Illustrative Example

ItemValue (SAR)Percentage
Building value before finishing (accredited appraisal)3,000,00075%
Completion cost (engineering bill of quantities)1,000,00025%
Total4,000,000100%
Distribution

In this example, the owner owns 75% of the shares and the parent company owns 25%. When profit is realized, it is distributed in the same proportion after the company has first recovered its rights.

Liquidation Upon Completion of Sales

  • After all units are sold and profits are distributed, the subsidiary is liquidated and its capital is repaid.
  • No ongoing legal or financial obligation remains.
  • Each project is isolated in a separate legal entity, preventing commingling of assets between different owners' projects.

5. Legal Structure and Project Companies (SPV)

Why a Special Purpose Vehicle (SPV)?

Bonds Financial Management & Consulting — through its parent company, Bond Holdings — has adopted an institutional model based on establishing a Special Purpose Vehicle (SPV) for each real estate project. The core idea is to isolate the project from the parent company and from the rest of the investment portfolio, providing transparency and a suitable environment for attracting additional financing.

Corporate Structure: Parent and Subsidiaries

Level One: The Parent Company

Bond Holdings — the administrative, financial, and strategic entity that holds the cumulative expertise in managing real estate projects.

Level Two: The Project Subsidiary (SPV)

A separate legal entity for each project, owning the real estate asset and contracting with contractors, suppliers, and tenants.

Distribution Mechanism in Detail

  1. Recovery of Parent Company Costs First: Net cash flows are directed to repay the cash actually paid by Bond Holdings for the project.
  2. 20% of Net Profit as Management Fee (Carried Interest): After full recovery, the parent company becomes entitled to 20% of the actual net profit achieved.
  3. Distribution of the Remainder According to Ownership Percentages: After cost recovery and deducting the 20% management fee, the remaining profits are distributed between the owner and the parent company according to their capital contribution ratios.

Available Partnership Options

ModelDescription
50/50 DistributionAfter cost recovery and deducting the 20% management fee, the remainder is split equally.
70/30 DistributionThe owner receives the larger share (70%) after cost recovery and the 20% management fee, while the parent company receives 30%.
Recovery + 20% OnlyThe parent company recovers its costs and then takes 20% of net profit, without claiming additional ownership.
Management for a Fixed FeeThe parent company manages the project for an annual management fee or a percentage of revenue, without entering as an equity partner.

Legal Risks and Safeguards

RiskSafeguard
Ownership Transfer RiskSigning a binding preliminary agreement with the owner, setting a transfer timeline, and calculating delay penalties.
Pre-existing Obligations RiskConducting comprehensive legal due diligence before signing, and requiring the owner to warrant bearing pre-existing obligations.
Municipal Approval RiskConsulting an accredited engineering office and obtaining preliminary approval before starting.
Piercing the Corporate Veil RiskSeparating accounts, contracts, and decisions, and avoiding commingling of project funds.
Partner Dispute RiskDrafting a precise partnership agreement containing clear decision-making mechanisms and dispute resolution procedures.

Proposed Financial Model

  • The owner contributes the property as an in-kind contribution to the project subsidiary (SPV).
  • Bond Holdings contributes the construction cost of SAR 7,252,000 as a cash contribution.
  • Revenues are distributed according to the mechanism: full recovery of the cash cost first, then 20% of net profit as a management fee, then distribution of the remainder according to the agreed ownership percentages.

Timeline

The project timeline is estimated at 6 to 8 months from site handover and issuance of required permits. The fast execution timeframe is a competitive advantage, as it reduces financing costs and allows faster cash conversion.

Lessons Learned

  1. Importance of Diversification: The mix of shops and apartments reduces reliance on a single income source.
  2. Value Added by Technology: Introducing smart home features is not a luxury, but a pricing differentiator.
  3. Need for Formal Real Estate Valuation: Indicative estimates alone should not be relied upon.
  4. Flexibility of the Legal Model: SPV enables project isolation and simplifies the relationship with the owner.
  5. Speed as a Success Factor: A compressed timeline enhances return attractiveness.

16.2 Recommendations

  1. Increase initial capital to SAR 60–70 million to cover operations for the first two years.
  2. Reduce number of shares or increase nominal value to avoid ownership fragmentation.
  3. Focus on Jeddah and Riyadh only in the first years.
  4. Build a database of distressed buildings before official launch.
  5. Partner with an accredited contractor who provides a bank guarantee for their work.
  6. Obtain project insurance against construction defects.
  7. Position Year 1 as a "foundation year" to investors instead of promising immediate profits.

16.3 Ideal Investor

  • Has a 5–7 year time horizon.
  • Understands that Years 1 and 2 are capacity-building years.
  • Appreciates the Mudarabah model as a smart alternative to land acquisition.
